Know every race on your ballot

A guide to Texas ballots in which every candidate, endorsement, and donation is tied to a public record you can open and read yourself.

Find the races on your ballot

Your address stays in your browser. It is sent to exactly one place — the U.S. Census Bureau’s free public geocoder at geocoding.geo.census.gov — which turns it into a map coordinate and returns it. It is never sent to this site, never stored, and never logged: this site is static files with no server and no database. Matching that coordinate to your precinct happens entirely on your own device.

Covers Tarrant, Dallas, and Collin counties so far. An address anywhere else in Texas will not match a precinct yet.

Your ballot is not the whole county

No races are listed yet, on purpose. Tarrant, Dallas, and Collin counties have … certified races between them for November 3, and no one votes in all of them — congressional, state legislative, State Board of Education, county commissioner, and justice of the peace seats are drawn by district. Enter your street address above and this page lists the races you are eligible to vote in, and nothing else.

Key dates

  • Oct 19–30 Early voting Monday, October 19 through Friday, October 30. In Tarrant County, any early voting site in the county will take your ballot, not just the one near your home; other counties set their own rule, so check with your county elections office.
  • Nov 3 Election day Tuesday, November 3. Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.

The last day to register is 30 days before the election. For November 3 that is October 4, which falls on a Sunday, so registrations are accepted through the next business day — Monday, October 5. Check your registration status at votetexas.gov.

How we source this

Every claim in this guide links to a public record. Endorsements point to the endorsing organization’s own announcement or to published news coverage. Donations point to the campaign finance report they were filed in. Nothing is included unless a link can be attached to it. Candidate information is never inferred, summarized, or ranked. The one exception is the short explanation of what each office does: those are plain-language summaries we wrote, and each links to the law it’s based on. If a line looks wrong, open its source and judge it yourself.
